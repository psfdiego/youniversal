1 Problems and assumptions

What problems do our users have?

Five problems the app could be hired to solve, phrased the way people describe them to us. Each card carries its role: a wedge problem makes someone choose this app over another, the retention problem brings them back, and the commodity is something every competitor already covers.

Why this comes firstA product is built around the problem it solves best, not around the features it could have. Listing the problems and ranking them is what let us narrow the original six-tab map down to something that can be validated. Until real users confirm them, these are assumptions.

2 User segments

Who has these problems, and how aware are they that our lens can help?

The same app has to serve someone who has never thought of their home as a lever and someone who practices Feng Shui daily. We sorted users by how aware they are of the problem and of our lens, using Eugene Schwartz's stages of awareness (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware), and found three groups that need different doors, different words and different proof.

Why segment before designingIt decides the first screen. Each group arrives for a different reason, so the app needs an entry for each. It also decides who to recruit for the beta: a test run only with practitioners would tell us what practitioners think, and nothing about the other two.
A
Problem-aware, lens-unaware
Feels stuck in one area, has tried practical fixes, has never considered the home or a chart as a lever. Reached through people (your content, a friend's shared card), not through search.

What this means for the app

The long-term positioning. Skeptical, so value has to land before any astrology term appears. Onboarding starts from the life area, never from the practice.
B
Solution-aware, curious
Searches "birth chart" or "feng shui bedroom". Curiosity is a job in itself (self-knowledge, identity, sharing), not the absence of a problem. Cheapest to acquire, highest churn.

What this means for the app

Designed for first: fastest to reach and to test. A full result in under a minute, shareable, and every curiosity result ends with a concern and one action, so there is a reason to return.
C
Practitioners
Reads a horoscope daily, sets new-moon intentions, likely follows you already. Wants accuracy, depth and one consistent school. Pays. Likely the beta group, so the beta must also recruit A and B outside your following.

What this means for the app

Accuracy is the trust moment. A wrong rising sign or a wrong trigram ends the session, so the app asks "does this look right?" before it asks for anything else.
Why we say "lens", not "cause"
  • Feng Shui and astrology are treated as a lens (a framework for reading a situation) and a practice (actions to take), never as a diagnosis of causes.
  • The app cannot claim that a room layout or a birth chart causes a life outcome. Causal wording fails advertising rules on unsubstantiated claims and App Store review, and it loses skeptics and practitioners alike.
  • Copy rule: no "causes", "because of" or "is why". Use "reads as", "suggests", "invites", "according to Feng Shui".
  • The mechanism that reliably delivers value is the physical action itself (clearing, rearranging, turning the bed), whatever the user believes about the tradition. Every screen is designed around the action, not the explanation.

Why write them this wayA job story names the moment a person reaches for the app. That moment is what onboarding, notifications and copy have to match. Features are easy to list; moments are what people actually experience.

4 Onboarding

How the app learns who it is talking to, and gives them something worth keeping in under a minute

Onboarding has one job: recognize which segment a person belongs to and hand them a first result before their patience runs out. It does that with a single question, then reads what they do. No account, no home profile, no quiz. And the first result is not a screen of the day: it is a full blueprint of the person, with the daily guidance offered right after.

Why a full blueprint first, then day to dayOne question is all we ask; a full report is what we give back. Sun, moon, Chinese sign and, from the trigram number, the four directions that support the person: where to sleep, where to sit, which way to face. It is personal, visual and shareable. Then, before the first Today screen, the person sees what else is here for them and why tomorrow will be different: the moon moves, the action changes, the cycle closes on the full moon.
Q1 and the first path
Q1: "What brings you here?"
Four goals and a skip; tapping an option advances. The screens walk through the first door, for a person who wants to change something in their life. The other doors follow below.

The question

  • Single choice, no continue button. Options are phrased as goals, never as self-labels: "How familiar are you with Feng Shui?" is not asked.
  • Each option carries a subtitle with its time cost. Curious users decide on that.
  • "Just looking" skips to a generic Today built on tonight's moon. Zero input, still something to read and share.

Birth data, one component everywhere

  • Date required, plus the answer the trigram formula needs (the traditional calculation differs for women and men; "show both" is a valid answer). Used for this and nothing else, and it says so.
  • Time and place as one optional group, collapsed behind "Know your birth time? Add it for your rising sign". They only add precision together.
  • Under the group: "Don't know it now? You can add it later in You."

The blueprint

  • The compass at the top is the memorable part: four directions that are yours, one for prosperity, health, relationships and sleep, from the Eight Mansions tables in your manual. The chart and the Chinese sign sit under it in three lines.
  • Each line expands; nothing else competes. Share sits in the top bar.
  • The report ends with one button: Guide me day to day.

The tour, then tonight's action

  • Five cards, one per thing the app does for them beyond the daily action: Space, Journal, Guide, People, and why come back tomorrow. Nothing to tap, nothing to set up.
  • Then the handover: tonight's first action, something physical the person can do before bed, and the other areas of the blueprint as chips under it. Every element says when it changes: the action tomorrow at 8:00, the cycle on the full moon in 14 days.
  • Account creation only when the user saves something. Never before the blueprint.

4 Onboarding · Flows by answer

Check a room: a verdict in three minutes, from eight questions

The second door. The room check reads the room itself, the way the bedroom chapter of your manual does: where the bed sits relative to the door, what faces it, what doesn't belong. No compass, no home profile, no photo. The home reading and the AI reimagining are offered once the room is saved, and they live in the Space tab (section 5).

Why the room comes before the homeCurious users arrive standing in a room they are rearranging. A room verdict in three minutes is the fastest proof the app can give. The home reading needs three things they may not have at hand (the facing side, its degrees, the year the roof went on), so it waits until the first room has earned the effort.

Any space, any goal

  • The picker is space-agnostic. Home, Work or Other is asked only on save, to file the room under a place.
  • "What should this room support?" offers all eight goals for every room (rest, love, calm, health, money, career, family, focus), with the ones most natural to that room first. Each maps to one of the personal directions, so a curious user becomes an intentional one without ever seeing the life question.

The eight bedroom questions

  • 1. Can you rearrange the furniture here? A no filters the actions to portable ones.
  • 2. Can you see the door from your bed? (the commanding position)
  • 3. Is there a solid wall behind the headboard?
  • 4. Is the bed under a window, or in line with the door?
  • 5. Does a mirror face the bed?
  • 6. What is under the bed?
  • 7. Is there a TV, a desk or exercise equipment in the room?
  • 8. Depends on the goal: for love, mementos of past relationships and paired objects; for rest and calm, light and noise; for health, electronics by the head; for money, career and focus, where work lives in the room.
  • Every question is one screen with an illustration and tap-to-answer options; the counter runs 1 to 8. The other rooms have their own sets, written with you from your chapters on kitchens, entrances, living rooms and desks.

The method, disclosed without a name

  • One line above the first question names the method, not the person: Classical Feng Shui, Flying Stars and Eight Mansions. "I practice a different school" logs a signal instead of opening a second rule base.

The verdict, and what comes after

  • A score, what works, what is worth a look, and three things to do ordered by effort, each with a "why". The verdict also opens the door to the person's own sleep direction.
  • Saving the room files it under a place and offers the next three moves: read your home, reimagine this room, and a re-check in seven days. The account is asked here, never before.

Understand my chart, Support a practice, and Just looking: the same blueprint, confirmed

The remaining doors. The curious want a portrait and something to share; practitioners want to verify before they trust anything; the person who taps "Just looking" wants to leave with something without giving anything. The first two get the same blueprint as door one, and the app asks whether it looks right before it asks for anything else.

Why ask "does this look right?"A practitioner who sees a wrong rising sign or a wrong trigram closes the app and does not come back. Confirming the blueprint costs one tap and turns the most demanding users into the most loyal ones.

Understand my chart

  • Date, place and time on one screen, with "I don't know" for the time, plus the trigram answer. Under a minute to the blueprint.
  • The blueprint opens onto the full astral chart (the wheel with planets, houses and aspects) for anyone who gave a birth time. It is part of the first version, in You.
  • Share card first, then "Save my card", then the account. Then the tour and "Guide me day to day", which lands on Today.

Support a practice I already have

  • The only multi-select in onboarding: daily horoscope, moon rituals or intentions, Feng Shui at home. It sets what loads first after the blueprint.
  • Birth data opens expanded, with time emphasized. Practitioners know it.
  • First practice by selection: Today from the full chart, the next moon event with "set your intention now", or Space with the home reading.

My card and the people around you

  • The card is the blueprint in one image: signs, trigram, sleep and work directions. Designed to be sent, not screenshotted.
  • From the card, "Improve a relationship" opens the people flow shown in the app section: not a compatibility score, but how two people's elements feed or drain each other, and what to do about it at home, at work and this week.

Just looking

  • Tonight's moon needs nobody's data. A theme, and the four doors as cards.
  • Quiet by default: no notifications until this user sets an intention or saves a room.

5 App structure

Five tabs, one job each: the app once someone is inside

Today for the habit, Space for the action, Journal for the two notes a day, Guide for the conversation, You for the memory. Explore and Ask from the original map are folded in: education lives inside every "why", and Ask became the Guide. Every card in the app says when its information will change, so people always know why to come back.

Why the Journal stands on its ownIt is the feature that brings people to the app twice a day, so it deserves its own door, not a corner of the chat. The notes stay private and on the phone; the Guide reads them to answer better, and Today reads them to choose the next action.

Today · the habit

  • Three blocks: moon, theme, one action with its "why". Done, not today or later; each is a signal. Under the action: new action tomorrow at 8:00.
  • The intention card shows the cycle and when it closes. On the day of the full moon it turns into "Close the cycle", the entry to the check-in.

Space · the action

  • Places hold rooms; rooms hold checks and scores with history and their re-check date. Read your home starts here. The reimagined rooms sit next to the rooms they came from.

Journal · two notes a day

  • Morning: what would make today feel right. Evening: what took your energy and what gave it back. Thirty seconds, typed or dictated with the phone keyboard, so no audio is stored.
  • A one-line reflection from the Guide under each note; the cycle's notes as a strip of days; the words that keep coming back. Everything stays on the phone, deletable one by one.

Guide · the conversation

  • Opens on what the journal said last, then answers anything from the person's own data and the rules, never from thin air. Suggested prompts keep it on rails. Not therapy, and it says so.

You · the memory

  • Blueprint, the full astral chart, my card, people, intentions with their closed cycles, settings.

5 App structure · Inside the tabs

The home reading, the astral chart, the people, the cycle, the reimagining

The deeper screens behind the five tabs. Two of them come straight from your manual: the facing of the home and this year's reading of its sectors. One is new since the last draft: the full astral chart, with planets, houses and aspects for anyone who gave a birth time.

The home, read your way

  • Sitting and facing, not the front door. The facing is the open, brighter, decorative side: the busiest street, the widest view, the balcony. The app asks with your clues, and the "which side would you photograph?" test.
  • Degrees from a map, the virtual reading your manual uses: an arrow from the quiet side to the facing side on the satellite view. The phone compass outside remains an option.
  • The year the roof went on sets the construction period; when unknown, the sitting number carries the reading, as your manual instructs.
  • Each sector shows a plain label and one remedy; the star numbers stay behind "Show the stars". The reading says until when it holds: 4 February 2027.

The full astral chart

  • The wheel with the twelve signs and houses, the planets in their places, and the aspects between them, in plain language. Houses need the birth time; without it the wheel shows planets in signs only.
  • Reached from the blueprint and from You. The astral chart of another person is one of the subscription moments, with an invitation as the free alternative (section 8).

People, the cycle, the reimagining

  • People: how the other person's element feeds or drains yours, what to do at home or at work, one thing this week, with a new one every Monday.
  • The check-in closes the cycle in three questions and names the day the next one starts.
  • Reimagining: the person's photo, rearranged along the verdict. Inspiration, clearly labelled; shareable.

9 The consultation questionnaire

Two audiences, two different forms

The home and business questionnaires are the practice's existing intake. They go to people who have already booked and paid, and they are due 48 hours before the appointment. The home form asks 49 questions across six sections; the business form mirrors it almost exactly. The instinct to cut them to three or four questions is right for one of these audiences and wrong for the other, and the app already solves its own half.

Why onboarding should not absorb these questionsSection 4 commits to one question, no account, no home profile and no quiz. It already defers the three inputs a home reading needs, the facing side, its degrees and the year the roof went on, until a first room check has earned the effort. That is the deferred profile, already designed in. The questionnaire belongs to a different person at a different moment: someone who has paid, and who will answer.
What the form is actually made of

The form is not heavy because it has 49 questions. Thirteen radio questions cost under a minute of tapping. Twelve required written answers cost the better part of an hour, and that is where people stall. The cut should be made by answer type, not by question count. Turn most of the written answers into taps with an optional "tell me more", and the same form takes about six minutes without losing a single input.

The life areas become taps

The deep material becomes the session
History of the house, favourite and least favourite rooms, what changed since moving in, how it feels to walk through the door. These are good questions asked at the wrong moment. They open a conversation, and they are worth more spoken than typed.

The four questions above are not chosen by taste. They are exactly the inputs the app needs in order to produce anything: birth dates give the trigram numbers, the address and the year give the flying star chart, the floorplan turns a list of sectors into an overlay, and the intention decides what the reading is for. Section 10 specifies what each of those results has to contain, and the home reading specification lists the same inputs from the other side.

Worth more attention than length
  • The waiver sits behind forty questions. Anyone who abandons the form partway has no signed waiver on file and may still arrive for the appointment. Consent should never depend on a client's patience for a questionnaire.
  • The two forms are maintained twice. Home and business overlap almost entirely and drift apart with every edit. One form that branches at the first question halves the upkeep and keeps the answers comparable.
  • Length may not be the real objection. The life-area block asks a stranger to rate their romantic life and their finances before they have met. If it underperforms, that is trust, not length, and shortening it will not fix it.
  • The app and the practice should share one profile. Birth data, floorplan and intention are asked in both places. Collected once and stored once, the consultation starts from what the app already knows.

Four decisions that should not wait for design

The astral chart

The full wheel: twelve signs, twelve houses, the planets in their places and the aspects between them. The most information-dense result in the app, and the one where a consumer audience and a practitioner audience pull hardest in opposite directions.

The wheel below is the document’s own example, computed rather than drawn from imagination: 14 March 1990, 07:35 CET, Valencia. Ascendant at the nine o’clock position, longitude increasing anticlockwise, the four angles marked, planet glyphs led back to their exact degree by a tick, major aspects as chords.
Conventions that are not optional
  • The ascendant sits at nine o’clock and longitude increases anticlockwise. Apparent daily motion runs the other way, which is why a planet rises on the left and sets on the right. Both are true; only the anticlockwise mapping matters to the renderer.
  • House spokes are not evenly spaced. In quadrant systems house size varies with latitude. Drawing twelve equal segments is wrong for every system except Whole Sign and Equal.
  • The midheaven is not always the tenth cusp. Under Whole Sign and Equal it detaches and can fall in another house entirely. Draw the midheaven and the lower meridian as independent markers, never derived from cusps ten and four.
  • Store absolute longitude, render sign and degree. Everything, aspects and rotation included, computes in one number from zero to 360. Sign, degree and minute are a display format.
What it shows
  • The wheel, with the sign ring, the house ring, the four angles, the planets and the aspect chords.
  • A placement list: each body with its sign, its house and its degree, expandable to a reading in plain language.
  • The aspects, in words rather than symbols, each naming the two bodies and what the tension or the ease is about.
  • Every element tappable. This is the one pattern common to every app that shows a real wheel. A dense wheel is usable only if touching any part of it explains that part.
Two strategies, and the recommendation

There are two proven approaches in the market. One reduces the wheel to a recognisable shape and puts all the precision in cards underneath it. The other draws a real wheel and treats it as a navigation surface with an explicit “explain this” affordance. The second fits this product better, because the practitioner segment is the one that pays and the document already commits to showing the method rather than hiding it. The reduced wheel remains the right choice for the share card, where the shape has to survive a thumbnail.

Do not aim at the professional ceiling. Arcsecond placements, minor aspects and a full aspect grid are where desktop astrology software lives. Naming the ceiling you stop short of is a scoping decision worth writing down.
Degradation

Set out in full in the birth-time specification. In short: without an accurate time, suppress the ascendant, the midheaven, every cusp and every house placement, and show the moon as a range rather than a point.

Decision · birth time and houses

Birth time, houses, and what to do without them

The single largest correctness risk in the astrology half of the app, and, on the evidence, an unoccupied gap in the market.

The document’s own example, recomputed at one-minute intervals. The ascendant crosses from Pisces into Aries between 07:35 and 07:36.
How fast the ascendant moves

The rule of thumb is one degree every four minutes, and as an average it is right: the meridian advances 15.041 degrees an hour. As a local figure it can be wrong by a factor of three. Signs rise at very different rates depending on latitude and on which sign it is. At the latitude of Paris the pair Pisces and Aries takes about an hour to rise while Virgo and Libra take nearly three. Further north the spread widens. A feature that reasons about birth-time uncertainty has to compute the actual local rate, not apply a constant.

The example in this document is a worked case of the worst version of this. At 07:35 the ascendant is at 29°59′ Pisces, roughly three arcminutes from Aries. It is moving at about 25 arcminutes per minute of clock time, so it sits within one degree of the boundary for under five minutes in total. A birth time remembered as “about half seven” produces a different rising sign. This is exactly the failure the document already names as fatal for the practitioner segment.
What is lost without an accurate time
CategoryWhat it covers
ImpossibleAscendant, midheaven, all twelve cusps, the house of every planet, and every lot. These are not degraded without a time, they are undefined.
UnreliableThe moon’s degree, and its sign on roughly two days in five. The moon moves about 13 degrees a day, near enough half a degree an hour.
UnaffectedThe sun’s sign except on a cusp day, every other planet’s sign, the aspects between them, and retrograde status.
Why a noon default is the wrong answer

Noon is the best single guess, because it minimises the worst-case error: the moon can be out by at most about seven degrees. But the ascendant can be out by up to half the zodiac. A rising sign derived from an assumed noon carries no information at all. Showing it anyway is not a small inaccuracy, it is a fabricated result on the one field the practitioner segment checks first.

The recommendation, and the differentiator. When the time is unknown: suppress the ascendant, the midheaven, the cusps and every house placement; show the moon as a range and, when it changes sign that day, show both signs; keep everything else. Every funded competitor surveyed either demands a time or tells the user to type noon themselves. Handling this honestly is both correct astrology and an unoccupied position.
The house system has to be chosen and named
SystemWhat it does
Whole SignHouse one is the whole sign containing the ascendant. Equal-sized houses, works at every latitude, and the system the traditional revival uses.
EqualThirty degree steps from the ascendant degree itself. Works at every latitude.
PlacidusDivides time rather than space. The most commonly used system in modern Western astrology, and the reason most wheels have unequal houses.
PorphyryTrisects each quadrant by longitude. Rarely chosen deliberately, and important because it is what libraries fall back to.
A concrete bug to avoid. Placidus and Koch cusps are undefined beyond the polar circle, at roughly 66.5 degrees, because some degrees never rise or set there and the arcs the method divides do not exist. The standard ephemeris library silently substitutes Porphyry and signals it only through a return code. An app that ignores that code will show Porphyry houses labelled Placidus to every user in Tromsø or Fairbanks. Whole Sign, Equal and Porphyry are defined everywhere.
The same example under two systems

With the ascendant at 29°59′ Pisces, the sun at 23°25′ Pisces falls in the twelfth house under Placidus and the first under Whole Sign. Same birth data, opposite reading. The app has to pick one, name it on screen, and stay with it.

Aspects are a data table, not constants

The five major aspects are fixed at zero, sixty, ninety, one hundred and twenty and one hundred and eighty degrees. The orbs are not fixed by anything. Published tables put the sextile anywhere from four to six degrees and the square from seven to ten, and most traditions widen the orb when the sun or the moon is involved, by amounts that also disagree. Two planets nine degrees apart are in conjunction under one table and unrelated under another. Ship the orbs as editable data, and name the table in the methodology screen.

Decision · the calculation engine

The ephemeris, and the licence that comes with it

A commercial decision disguised as a technical one. It should be settled before design, because it is cheap to resolve and expensive to discover late.

The situation

Every planetary position in the app comes from an ephemeris. The standard one in astrology software is the Swiss Ephemeris, which agrees with NASA’s reference data to a thousandth of an arcsecond and solves every house system including the awkward ones. It is dual licensed. Either the whole project is released under the AGPL, a copyleft licence that would require publishing the app’s source, or a professional licence is bought.

The numbers

Two traps worth knowing before anyone picks the free option. First, moving the calculation to your own server does not avoid the licence; Astrodienst’s contract treats an app that calls a server running the Swiss Ephemeris as an app containing it. Second, copyleft licences and Apple’s App Store terms are widely understood to conflict, which is why at least one well known application was removed from the store over it. For a paid iOS product, “just comply with the AGPL” is not a route. Worth a short conversation with counsel rather than a decision made in a sprint.
Recommendation. Buy the professional licence. Seven hundred Swiss francs once is far less than the engineering cost of reimplementing house cusps on a permissive engine, and that reimplementation is exactly where the polar-circle edge cases live. If the licence is refused for other reasons, astronomy-engine is a real alternative, but budget the cusp work honestly.
One accuracy risk that is easy to miss

Turning a birth time into a position means turning local time into universal time, and that depends on what the time zone and the daylight-saving rule actually were at that place on that date. The open time zone database warns in its own documentation that many of its pre-1970 entries are wrong or misleading, and its values for the era before standard time are an invention of the database rather than a record. Every user born before about 1970, which is a large share of the practitioner segment, sits in that window. Astrodienst maintains its own historical time zone data precisely because of this, which is part of what the licence fee buys and part of what building on a free engine would leave to be solved.

Decision · the symbol system

Every symbol the app draws

The app needs roughly sixty marks across two traditions. None of them can be typed as text, which is a finding rather than an opinion, and it changes the asset plan.

The twelve zodiac signs and the ten planets, drawn as vector paths for this document. Every symbol in the app should be produced this way.
Why not just use the characters

Unicode encodes the twelve signs and most of the planets, so the obvious move is to type them. It does not work, for four separate reasons, all verified rather than assumed.

  • The signs are classified as emoji. All twelve carry a property that asks for a colour emoji presentation by default. On Apple platforms that is exactly what happens.
  • The system interface font contains none of them. Not one sign, not one planet. Every symbol falls back to some other font whose weight and baseline nobody chose. In practice a single chart legend can render in three typefaces at once: colour emoji signs, monochrome symbol planets, and ordinary text.
  • Three of the marks do not exist as characters at all. There is no retrograde character, no trine and no square. Everyone borrows a pharmacy symbol and two geometric shapes from an unrelated block, which will never optically match the rest.
  • Two planets are misnamed. The characters used for Venus and Mars are formally the female and male signs. A screen reader announces them that way.
The consequence for this document. The wheel in the astral chart specification was first built with Unicode characters and rendered as purple emoji blocks. The version shown there is drawn as paths. That is the same decision the product has to make.

How a home reading is built

A star is not permanently good or bad; it is timely or it is not. The 8 was the prosperity star for the whole of 2004 to 2023 and is now retreating, and the 9 has taken its place. Any interpretation table written as fixed meanings will read as dated to a practitioner within a year. Build the timeliness of each star as data keyed to the period, not as prose.
Why the app must ask for degrees

Three hundred and sixty degrees divide into twenty-four mountains of fifteen degrees each, three to each of the eight directions. The polarity that decides which way the stars fly can change between one mountain and the next inside the same direction. Two homes that both “face north”, one at 345 degrees and one at 0 degrees, produce different charts. This is the reason the facing capture in the app is an arrow on a satellite view rather than a list of eight buttons, and it is worth saying so on the screen.

The year turns at Li Chun, not at Chinese New Year

The annual star changes at the start of the solar year, the first of the twenty-four solar terms, which falls on the third, fourth or fifth of February. Chinese New Year is a different date and can be five weeks away. Compute the moment astronomically rather than hard-coding the fourth of February, because the reading’s expiry date is shown on screen and is the yearly reason to return.

Remedies follow the element cycles, in one specific direction

The two problem stars, the 5 Yellow and the 2 Black, are both Earth. Earth generates Metal, so Metal drains Earth, which is why the traditional remedy is metal. It is worth being precise about this in any explanatory copy: Metal does not control Earth. Wood controls Earth, and classical practice deliberately avoids using Wood against the 5, on the grounds that it provokes rather than settles. Getting this backwards in a “why” line is the kind of error the practitioner audience notices immediately.

Remedies are quantified, and this changes the product

The most consequential thing on the sheet. Her remedies are not “add a metal bowl”, they are weights and volumes calculated from the size of the building.

  • Metal. About three per cent of the square footage in pounds, or ten per cent of the square metres in kilos, with a floor of thirty pounds or fifteen kilos. Method · Eight Mansions

The personal compass

The calculation behind the most memorable element in the app. It is small, it is fast, and it has two edge cases that will produce wrong answers for real users if they are not handled.

The personal compass for trigram number 1, the example used throughout this document. Four directions support and four do not, and the set flips entirely between the east and west groups.
What it is

A number from one to nine derived from the birth year and the sex the traditional formula asks for. The number carries an element, a trigram, and membership of the east or the west group. From it come four supportive directions and four to avoid. The four that support one group are exactly the four the other group avoids, which is what makes the two-person reading work.

The calculation, from her manual

Her workbook gives a single formula that works in any century, with no special case per hundred years.

Step
FirstIf the birth date falls between 1 January and 4 or 5 February, use the previous year. This is the solar boundary, not Chinese New Year.
ThenAdd all four digits of the year and reduce to a single digit.
MenSubtract that from 11. A result of 5 becomes 2.
WomenAdd 4 and reduce again. A result of 5 becomes 8.
Use hers, not the one in circulation. The widely published version works on the last two digits and subtracts from 10 or adds 5. For most years the two agree. For men born in 2009, 2018 or 2027 the published version returns zero, which is not a trigram at all; hers returns 9, which is what her own printed chart shows. Implement hers and test against her chart, which covers 1920 to 2030.

CaseWhat to do
The number 5Five sits at the centre of the grid and has no trigram and no direction, so it has to be substituted. The common convention gives men the number 2 and women the number 8. Decision · the school

Declaring one school, and living with it

The document already commits to Classical Feng Shui, read from Flying Stars and Eight Mansions. This is what that commitment rules out, and the list of smaller choices that follow from it.

The two cycles every remedy in the app depends on. Generating, where each element feeds the next, and controlling, where each checks another. A third relationship, draining, is the generating cycle read backwards, and it is the one the metal remedies rely on.
The incompatibility
SchoolHow it reads a room
Classical, compass-basedThe grid is laid on real compass bearings taken to the degree. Time matters: periods, annual stars. The person matters: the trigram number. This is the practitioner’s method and the one the app follows.
Western, front-door-basedThe grid is aligned to the wall the front door sits in, and no compass is used at all. Time and the person are largely absent. Remedies are symbolic objects.
These cannot be mixed, and the reason is mechanical rather than political. Flying Stars are derived from a compass bearing. Laying them over a grid that was aligned to a doorway produces numbers in the wrong rooms. Any feature that borrows a familiar Western layout because users have seen it elsewhere breaks the readings everywhere else in the app.
What the app therefore owes the user
  • A methodology screen naming the school, the boundary conventions, the substitutions and the direction of north used. Practitioners respect a stated position far more than a system that silently picks one.
  • One line above the first room question naming the method rather than the person, which the document already specifies.
  • A logged signal, not a second rule base, when someone says they practise differently. Supporting two schools doubles the content and halves the credibility.
  • Consistent language. A room reads as restless; it does not cause anything. The copy rule from the segments section applies hardest in the Feng Shui half of the app.
The five elements, and the direction that matters

Two cycles do most of the work: generating, where each element feeds the next, and controlling, where each checks another. The app also needs the third relationship, draining, which is simply the generating cycle read backwards: if Earth generates Metal, then Metal exhausts Earth. Almost every remedy in classical practice is a draining move rather than a controlling one, and explanatory copy that says “controls” where it means “drains” will read as wrong to anyone trained.
